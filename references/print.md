# Print: posters, flyers, brochures, documents

## Two shapes

- **Fixed pagination** - posters, flyers, brochures, one-page docs: a **series of single-page
  artboards**, one per page.
- **Flowing document** - memos, reports: a **single tall artboard** that paginates on export.

Never paginate a print piece by putting pages on separate canvas pages. Pages of one piece belong
side by side on one canvas page, in reading order.

## Author at 96 px per inch

| Size | Pixels at 96 ppi |
|---|---|
| A4 | 794 × 1123 |
| Letter | 816 × 1056 |
| Tabloid | 1056 × 1632 |
| A5 | 559 × 794 |

At that scale **12pt = 16px**, which is the floor for reading copy. Short labels and legal lines
may go to 12px, nothing goes below. Size headlines with ~10% slack, because export shows the
fallback face, not the webfont.

## A flier is read in under three seconds

Read at a distance, in passing, by someone who did not choose to read it.

- **One dominant element** - usually a headline under six words, sized to read across a room
  (60pt+). Everything else clearly subordinate.
- **Group the five Ws tight and scannable**: what, when, where, cost, and one way to act. Not
  scattered through prose.
- **Flat color blocks and vector shapes** over photos and gradients. High contrast.
- **Generous whitespace beats more words.** Cut copy until the hierarchy is unmissable.
- **Check it in grayscale.** If the hierarchy collapses, the color was doing work the layout
  should be doing.

## Trifold panel order is fold order

This is where trifolds go wrong. On the **outside** face, left to right: inside flap, back cover,
**front cover**. The cover is the rightmost panel. The **inside** face reads as one three-panel
spread.

Write content to unfold in the order the reader experiences it: the cover makes one promise, the
inside delivers it in three readable beats, the back carries logistics and contact.

## Print discipline

- Think in physical units. A design that only works at screen scale is not a print design.
- No hairlines. Anything under ~0.5pt vanishes on paper.
- No huge dark flood fills - they drink ink, cockle the paper, and look muddy off a home printer.
- Bleed matters if it will actually be printed: ask, and add 3mm (≈11px at 96 ppi) if so.
